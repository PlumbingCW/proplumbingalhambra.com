Emergency Faucet Repair in Alhambra, CA

Written by the Alhambra Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Faucet repair in Alhambra fixes leaking spouts, dripping handles, low pressure and valves that will not shut off. A dripping faucet wastes water and stains the sink, and a valve that will not close can turn into an emergency, so the same plumbers who handle burst pipes handle fixtures.

When a faucet or valve is an emergency

A faucet or shower valve that will not turn off, or a supply line that is spraying, needs quick action. Call the 24/7 line, and while a plumber is on the way:

What to do while you wait

  1. Close the small angle stop valve under the sink or behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  2. If there is no local valve or it will not close, shut off the main water valve to the house.
  3. Open another faucet lower in the house to relieve pressure.
  4. Place towels and a bucket under the leak, and clear the cabinet if water is pooling inside.

Why faucets fail

Most drips come from a worn cartridge, washer, O-ring or seat inside the faucet. Alhambra's hard water speeds this up. The City's 2024 water quality report records hardness at 202 ppm in groundwater and 272 ppm in imported water. Scale collects on aerators, valve seats and cartridges, so flow drops and seals wear.

A faucet that is more than ten years old and has been repaired before is usually better replaced than rebuilt. A faucet with a corroded body or loose mounting is the same.

Shower and tub valves

A shower that keeps dripping after it is off wastes water and can mean the valve is on its way out. Behind the wall sits a cartridge or a valve body, and which one depends on how old the house is. Houses from the 1930s and 1940s may have original valves that need parts that are no longer made, in which case the plumber replaces the valve body.

Access is the main variable. If the shower has an access panel, valve work is a small job. If not, the plumber may open a small section of wall and restore it afterward. We explain which it will be before work starts.

What we check on a fixture visit

  • ✓The angle stops and supply lines, which are often the weak part of the system.
  • ✓Water pressure at the fixture, and whether the house pressure is within a normal range.
  • ✓Drain connections and traps under the sink.
  • ✓Signs of a hidden leak behind the cabinet or in the wall.
  • ✓Fixture mounting, so the faucet does not rock or leak at the deck.

If low pressure affects the whole house and not one faucet, the cause may be a leak or a failing pressure regulator, and leak detection is the next step. If the problem is a pipe and not a fixture, burst pipe repair covers the response.

Supply valves, tub spouts and shower leaks

The small shutoff valves under sinks and behind toilets are used rarely and fail when they are needed most. A valve that will not close, or that leaks at the stem, should be replaced before it is a problem. A supply line that bursts sends water at full house pressure, which is why a stiff valve deserves attention now.

A tub spout that drips or a showerhead that never fully stops is wasting water and usually has a worn diverter or cartridge. A tub that drains slowly often has a hair clog in the drain, and a plumber can clear it without removing the tub. When the drain is slow and the shower pan also feels damp, ask for a leak check.

What shapes the price

Faucet and fixture pricing depends on:

Repair or replace
A cartridge swap is a small job. A new faucet plus supply lines takes more time.
Fixture type
Kitchen pull-downs and tub-shower valves involve different labor.
Access
A shower valve behind a tiled wall costs more than a sink faucet.
Parts availability
Older faucets may need a full replacement.
Hour of the call
A night call costs more than a daytime appointment.

You get a clear price before work starts. Homeowners insurance usually responds to sudden water damage but often excludes slow leaks and wear. Ask your carrier before work starts and keep the photos and the plumber's written findings.
