Plumber in Midwick Tract, Alhambra, CA

Written by the Alhambra Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Midwick Tract is the southwest corner of Alhambra, built on the former Midwick Country Club grounds. This page is for homeowners there who want plumbing help and a few plain facts about the area.

Where Midwick Tract sits

Midwick Tract lies in the southwest corner of Alhambra, according to the Discover Alhambra History neighborhood guide. It is named for the Midwick Country Club that once occupied the land. A Realtor neighborhood guide places Granada Park just to the north and Interstate 10 nearby, and lists the area in ZIP code 91803.

Streets in the tract bend and end in cul-de-sacs instead of running in a straight grid. When you call, give the dispatcher your street name and the nearest cross street, because house numbers alone can be hard to place on curving roads.

Homes in the tract

The tract was developed in the 1940s. The local history guide describes Spanish Colonial Revival, Mid-Century Modern and Minimal Traditional houses. One real estate guide reports a median build year of 1949 and a typical lot of about 7,000 square feet, though those figures come from listings and not from the city.

Style and age tell you little about the pipes inside any one house. Remodels over the decades may have replaced some lines and left others alone. A plumber who opens a wall or runs a camera will tell you what your house really has.

Plumbing jobs that suit single-story homes

Single-story houses keep most of their plumbing on one level. That makes some work simple to reach and some leaks easy to miss. A slow leak under a slab or behind a closet wall can run for weeks before a stain shows.

Five checks a Midwick Tract homeowner can do in ten minutes

  • ✓Find the main shutoff valve and turn it by hand once a year so it does not seize.
  • ✓Look at the water heater floor and the pan underneath for rust or a damp ring.
  • ✓Read the water meter, stop all water use for 15 minutes and read it again.
  • ✓Check the hose bibs for drips and wet soil, and look under every sink for damp cabinet floors.
  • ✓Run each drain for a minute and listen for gurgling that points to a partial blockage.

Found something on that list? Call and describe what you see.
