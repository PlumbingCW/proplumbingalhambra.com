What Is Hydro Jetting a Sewer Line in Alhambra, CA?

Written by the Alhambra Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

Hydro jetting a sewer line means cleaning the pipe that leaves your house with pressurized water. A plumber feeds a hose with a special nozzle through the cleanout, and the nozzle sprays water in the pipe to cut away buildup and flush it downstream. It is used for grease, scale, silt and small roots. It is not a repair, so the pipe is inspected first, and anything cracked or collapsed is fixed another way.

How the process works

The sewer lateral is the pipe that carries all wastewater from the house to the public sewer. If it clogs, every fixture is affected, and the lowest drains back up first. A plumber accesses the lateral through a cleanout, a capped opening that is often in the yard, the side of the house or the basement.

The jetting hose has a nozzle with several holes. Forward jets break up the blockage. Backward jets pull the hose through the pipe and scour the walls. The plumber works along the pipe in passes, then checks the result with a camera.

Hydro jetting a sewer line, step by step

  1. Locate the cleanout and confirm the line direction.
  2. Run a camera to see the pipe condition and the blockage.
  3. Choose a nozzle and a pressure that suit the pipe material.
  4. Jet the line in passes until the camera shows a clean wall.
  5. Run the camera again, record the footage and discuss the results.

The footage is the useful part. It tells you whether the clog was a one-off, whether it will return and whether you need a repair. If the pipe has a crack or a broken joint, jetting can push water into the soil, so the plumber should tell you before going further. See sewer line repair for the options.

Questions to ask a plumber about jetting

  • ✓Will you run a camera before and after?
  • ✓What pressure will you use on my pipe?
  • ✓Can I keep the footage?
  • ✓What do you do if you find a crack?
  • ✓How often should I expect to repeat this?
  • ✓Do you also clear the line with a cable if needed?

Emergency or not?

Sewage coming up in a tub or shower, or any backup in the lowest drain with the whole house affected, is an emergency. Stop using water and call. A slow main line with no overflow can be scheduled, but do not wait many days. See hydro jetting for emergency response.

Jetting versus lining and replacement

Cleaning and repair are different jobs. Jetting clears the inside of a pipe that is structurally sound. Lining places a cured resin sleeve inside a cracked pipe to seal it without a full trench. Replacement removes the old pipe and installs a new one, either by digging or by pulling a new pipe through the old path.

The camera tells you which path is right. A pipe with only buildup is a jetting job. A pipe with a few cracks or open joints may suit spot repair or lining. A pipe that has collapsed, bellied heavily or lost long sections usually needs replacement. A fair quote shows the footage that supports each choice, and you should be able to keep it.

Common myths about sewer jetting

What people get wrong

  • ✓Myth: jetting breaks old pipes. Fact: a pipe in sound condition handles proper pressure, and a camera check first prevents damage.
  • ✓Myth: one jetting lasts forever. Fact: it removes buildup, and the causes can return.
  • ✓Myth: a snake and a jet are the same. Fact: they work in different ways.
  • ✓Myth: jetting fixes a bad slope. Fact: a belly holds water and needs repair.

What to expect on the day

Plan on one to three hours for a typical house line. The plumber needs access to the cleanout, so clear the path and secure pets. Water use in the house should stay light while the line is open, because wastewater can back up if the line is only partly clear.

When the work is done, you should hear what was found, see the footage and get a plain recommendation. If the line is clear and sound, that is the end of it. If not, you will get options with prices before anything else happens.

Safety checklist: what to do before and during a sewer visit

  • ✓Keep children and pets away from the cleanout and the hose.
  • ✓Do not use drain chemicals before the plumber arrives, because they can burn skin.
  • ✓Wear gloves if you touch wastewater and wash your hands afterward.
  • ✓Keep the work area clear of cars and trip hazards.
  • ✓Report any sewage smell indoors, since it can mean a blockage or a dry trap.
