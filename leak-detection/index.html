Emergency Leak Detection in Alhambra, CA

Written by the Alhambra Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Leak detection in Alhambra finds the exact spot where water is escaping, without cutting open walls and floors to look. Whether your meter keeps turning with the taps off, a ceiling is stained, or the water bill jumped, a licensed plumber uses listening, pressure and thermal tools to locate the leak before any repair is quoted.

When a hidden leak is an emergency

Water through a ceiling, a wet wall or an active spray cannot wait for a business day. Call our 24/7 line. While you wait:

What to do while you wait

  1. Shut off the main water valve to the house, usually where the supply line enters near the front, by turning it clockwise.
  2. Turn off power to any room with wet ceilings or walls if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  3. Move belongings out of the wet zone and place buckets where water is dripping.
  4. Photograph the damage and the meter reading before and after you shut the valve.

How we find a leak without tearing up the house

The plumber starts with the meter and isolation tests to confirm there is a leak and which line it is on. If the meter spins with everything off and the yard is dry, the leak may be inside the slab or walls. Acoustic listening devices pick up the sound water makes escaping from a pressurized pipe. Tracer gas or thermal imaging can confirm the position.

The result is a mark on the floor or wall, within a small area, so the repair opens a small patch instead of a room. You also get a plain explanation of what was found and what the repair needs.

High bill or running meter: start with the city meter

If your water bill jumped, the City of Alhambra Utilities Department is the billing authority. You can reach it at (626) 570-5061 at 111 S. First Street, Monday through Thursday. Ask whether the usage pattern shows continuous flow. That is a sign of a leak and is worth telling the plumber.

Many high bills come from toilets that run, irrigation lines or a water heater that leaks into a drain. Part of leak detection is ruling those out, and that is cheaper than opening a wall.

Do this before the plumber arrives

  • ✓Turn off every tap, appliance and irrigation timer, then check whether the meter is still turning.
  • ✓Note the meter reading, wait 30 minutes with nothing running, and read it again.
  • ✓Look for a toilet that keeps refilling. Add food coloring to the tank and see if it shows in the bowl.
  • ✓Check the area around the water heater and under sinks for dampness.
  • ✓Write down when you first noticed the sign. It helps the plumber narrow the search.

What the leak may mean for older Alhambra homes

In the older tracts, such as Mayfair and Bean with homes built in the 1930s and 1940s, original galvanized or early copper supply lines can corrode and develop pinhole leaks. Postwar homes like those in the Airport Tract may have copper lines in or under a slab. Alhambra's very hard water leaves scale that does not stop a leak but can make pipe walls rougher and valves stiffer.

If detection finds that a pipe is failing along its length, the choice may move from spot repair to repiping. You make that decision with the findings in hand. For a burst or spraying pipe, go straight to burst pipe repair.

If a water bill has jumped, the meter test on the Mayfair Tract page walks through it step by step.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

A stain on a ceiling is the end of a trip the water took, not the start. Water can run along a joist or down a pipe for several feet before it shows. The plumber works backward from the stain, checking the bathroom or kitchen above, the supply lines in the attic and the roof penetrations, and uses a moisture meter and a thermal camera to follow the trail.

Do not paint over a stain until the source is found. Paint hides the damage and traps moisture. If the drywall is soft or the stain is growing, treat it as an active leak and shut off the main while you wait.

Under-house and crawlspace leaks

Houses on raised foundations hide their plumbing under the floor, where a slow leak can soak insulation, rot framing and feed mold for months. Signs are a musty smell, a floor that feels springy, a meter that moves when everything is off and rising humidity in the house.

Access matters. Some crawlspaces have a hatch and room to work, and others are tight. The plumber checks the supply lines, the drain connections and the water heater feed, and tells you what drying or insulation work may follow. We do not do mold remediation, and we say so when it is needed.

Buried service lines, yard leaks and meter boxes

The water service line runs from the meter to the house, often underground and often older than anything inside. A leak here can show up as a soggy patch, a lower-than-normal pressure or a meter that spins with the house shut off. Meter-box leaks sit at the connection and are sometimes the utility's side, so look at the meter and call the City if the leak is at or before it.

Irrigation lines leak at valves and heads and can look like a service line leak. Pool and spa plumbing is a separate system with its own specialists, and we will say honestly when a pool leak needs a pool company. For a buried line that has failed, the options are repair at the break, a new line or rerouting, which we discuss with you before digging.

Low water pressure

Low pressure in one fixture usually means a clogged aerator or valve. Low pressure everywhere points to a partly closed main valve, a failing pressure regulator, a corroded supply line or a leak. Compare hot and cold, because low pressure on hot only suggests the water heater or its lines.

Test the pressure at an outside spigot with a gauge. Most homes sit in a moderate range. A reading far outside it justifies a regulator check. If your pressure drops over months and the house has old galvanized pipe, repiping may be the lasting fix.
