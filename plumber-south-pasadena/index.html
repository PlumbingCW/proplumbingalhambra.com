The problems South Pasadena homeowners call about first

Plumbing trouble in South Pasadena usually starts with water in a place it should not be. A ceiling stain under a bathroom, a wet spot on a hardwood floor, a meter that keeps turning when every tap is off. Those symptoms point at a hidden supply leak, and the first step is finding it before anything is opened. Leak detection uses acoustic listening and thermal imaging so the plumber opens one small patch of wall and not the whole room.

Slow drains come next. Kitchen sinks that gurgle and tubs that drain in a lazy spiral are usually a partial blockage in the branch line. Older sewer laterals made of clay or cast iron take roots over the years, and a camera pass shows the difference between grease, roots and a broken joint. What to do while you wait

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It is usually at the front of the house near the meter or where the line enters the garage.
  2. Switch off power to wet areas at the breaker if water is near outlets or light fixtures.
  3. Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the water and open a window if you can.
  4. Take a photo of the damage for your insurance carrier, then call our 24/7 line.

What the City's water report means for your plumbing

South Pasadena runs its own wells in the Main San Gabriel Groundwater Basin and also receives imported water from the Metropolitan Water District. The City's water quality report lists groundwater hardness at 150 mg/L, imported Metropolitan water at 230 mg/L and the Pasadena zone at 220 mg/L. Hardness at that level leaves scale on fixtures and inside water heaters over time.

The report also says the City's wells use free chlorine while Metropolitan water is treated with chloramines. Which one reaches your tap depends on where your street sits. Ask the Public Works Department at 626-403-7240 which supply serves your address, and read the report itself for current numbers. This is the kind of detail that helps when you decide whether a water heater repair or a descaling visit makes sense.

Renters, owners and who calls whom

More than half of South Pasadena households rent, according to the 2020 census figures for the city. If you rent, a leak or a dead water heater is a call to your landlord first, but many property managers want a licensed plumber on site fast and will approve an emergency visit. We give the written findings and photos you need for that conversation.

Owners of older homes should ask about pipe material when they buy or remodel. Galvanized supply lines, clay laterals and original shutoff valves are the usual weak points. A sewer inspection with a camera before a purchase is cheaper than a surprise after closing.
