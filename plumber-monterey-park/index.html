Hills, flats and the freeway corridors

Monterey Park is split by geography. The Garvey and Monterey Hills once formed a natural barrier between the northern and southern parts of the city, and the layout still shapes where pipes run and how deep they sit. Homes on slopes often have long drain runs with a steep fall, while flat streets near the I-710, I-10 and SR 60 freeway ramps have shallower, slower lines.

Slope matters for plumbing. On a hillside, water pressure at the lowest fixture can be high enough to stress old supply lines, and a pressure regulator check is cheap insurance. On the flats, slow drains and sewer backups are more common because the pipe has less fall to carry waste away.

  1. Close the main water valve. In hillside homes it is often at the street side of the lot, near the meter.

  3. Move valuables and stock off the floor, and block off the area if you run a business.

Shopping centers, restaurants and shared buildings

Monterey Park has real commercial density. The Monterey Park Marketplace covers about 45 acres along the Pomona Freeway, Atlantic Times Square opened in 2010 and the city has a long run of restaurants and offices. Food service drains clog with grease, floor drains back up, and restroom problems shut a business down within hours.

Commercial plumbing here works differently from a house call. We ask whether you are open, who owns the line and whether the problem is inside your suite or in a shared building drain. For repeat grease clogs, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall instead of punching a hole through the blockage.

Where the water comes from

Monterey Park runs its own water system. The City says it operates 12 deep wells near the Rio Hondo River and is upgrading its Delta treatment facility in Rosemead. Its water system serves over 95 percent of residents and businesses, and private water companies serve the rest. The City's page does not state hardness, so test your tap or ask the Water Division at 626-307-1285.

For a water main problem on the street side of your meter, the City is the right call, and its after-hours emergency line is 626-573-1311. We handle everything on your side of the meter, from a leaking shutoff valve to a burst line in the yard. See leak detection if you are not sure which side the leak is on.

How to tell whose problem it is

  • ✓Look at the meter. Water use with every tap off means a leak somewhere after the meter.
  • ✓A wet spot near the sidewalk may be the City's line, so photograph it and call the Water Division.
  • ✓Water inside the house is yours. Shut the main and call us.
  • ✓A sewer backup in several fixtures points at the lateral, and a camera tells you where.
  • ✓If you rent, tell the owner, but do not wait to shut the water off.
