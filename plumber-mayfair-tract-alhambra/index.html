Plumber in Mayfair Tract, Alhambra, CA

Written by the Alhambra Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Mayfair Tract is in southern Alhambra and is known for its 1930s revival-style homes. This page helps homeowners track down hidden leaks and sudden jumps in the water bill.

Where Mayfair Tract sits

Mayfair Tract is in southern Alhambra, according to the Discover Alhambra History neighborhood guide. The same guide calls it Alhambra's most intact original neighborhood and dates it to the 1930s. Some directories label it Mayfair Park. Give the dispatcher your street and cross street so there is no confusion.

Homes in the tract

Local sources describe Tudor Revival, Spanish Colonial Revival, Cape Cod Revival and Storybook houses here. A neighborhood that has kept its original character also tends to keep original layouts, which can make it hard to guess where a hidden pipe runs.

A jump in the water bill

A higher bill does not always mean a leak. Guests, a new irrigation schedule and hot weather all raise use. A leak is likely when the meter keeps moving after every tap and appliance is off.

A simple meter test

  1. Turn off every tap, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the sprinklers.
  2. Open the meter box and find the small leak indicator or the dial.
  3. Write down the reading, wait 15 to 30 minutes and read it again.
  4. If the dial moved, close the main valve and read again to see whether the leak is inside or outside the house.
  5. If it still moves with the main closed, the leak is on the street side. Call the City utility.

If the meter stops when you close the main, the leak is inside your own pipes. Leak detection finds it with listening and thermal tools. If it moves with the main closed, the City's Utilities office at (626) 570-5061 handles the supply side.

What water Alhambra delivers

The City of Alhambra's 2024 water quality report says about 70 percent of its water comes from local groundwater and about 30 percent is imported from the Metropolitan Water District. Hardness differs between the two sources. Hard water leaves scale on fixtures and heaters, which is a general issue and not a finding about any one street.
